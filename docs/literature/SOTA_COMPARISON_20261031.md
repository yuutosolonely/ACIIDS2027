# CRIC-ED: comparison with closest methods and SOTA claims

**Review date:** 2026-10-31  
**Project:** CRIC-ED / TabFM-ED  
**Status:** targeted closest-work comparison; not an exhaustive systematic review, reproduced leaderboard, or novelty proof.

> **Update 2026-10-31 (later):** the confirmatory analysis is now complete and entered in `docs/CLAIM_EVIDENCE.md` (CLM-10..20); purification was worse than matched random removal on 3 of 4 real tables, so no superiority claim arises. Auto-Test and MaTElDa identities/method framing were re-checked from their primary PDFs; LUCARIO and Cell-TRICC only from Crossref metadata (+ LUCARIO repository README) because publisher pages returned 403. The paper cites these four with sentences limited to what was checked.

## Executive assessment

The project has compared its proposed task and method with a meaningful set of direct and adjacent work. It has **not** established that it outperforms state of the art (SOTA): the cited methods do not all solve the same prediction task, the present protocol does not reproduce several direct methods, and the confirmatory benchmark results have not yet been accepted as complete evidence in the claim ledger.

The broad ideas below are already present in prior work and must not be claimed as new:

- Tabular foundation models (TFMs) for conditional prediction, density/anomaly scoring, or data cleaning.
- Conditional reconstruction of a feature from the other features.
- Contaminated reference/context effects in TFM anomaly detection.
- Cross-fitting or leave-one-out techniques by themselves.
- Label-free or learned context selection in general.
- Benefits of cleaner contexts in general.

The narrow candidate delta is an **evaluation of one fixed, target-specific context-row purification rule for unsupervised cell-error ranking**, with nested outer-fold isolation and a matched-count random-removal control. The protocol also records support, failures, rare-valid alarms, boundary sensitivity, and added compute. This is an incremental, protocol-and-evidence contribution unless the completed results establish a stronger methodological effect. No first-of-its-kind claim is supported by this scan. The first version omitted several directly relevant lines of work—among them Uni-Detect, Auto-Test, MaTElDa, LUCARIO, and Cell-TRICC—now added below. Treat this as evidence that the scan is targeted, not exhaustive.

## Task definitions: what can be compared

| Task | Output and labels | Examples | Comparable to CRIC-ED cell AP? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cell error detection (Cell-ED) | A score for each table cell; often dirty/clean disagreement labels | Auto-Test, LUCARIO, ZeroED, ForestED, Raha, MaTElDa, Tab-Cleaner, DDC-family methods | Potentially, only after aligning rows, eligible cells, label definition, split, and metric. |
| Row anomaly detection | One anomaly score per row; anomalies may be whole records or unusual feature combinations | TACTIC, ZEN/FOCUS, TaskBridge, RFOD, SPCAD | No. A row AUROC/FPR is not cell AP; an intermediate cell score does not make the reported endpoint Cell-ED. |
| Label-error detection / context attribution | Detect errors in a designated target label, often with labels or supervised tasks | TICDA | No direct score comparison: target and supervision differ. |
| Cleaning for downstream prediction | Select or execute transformations to improve a downstream TFM | L2C2 and related cleaning-policy work | No direct Cell-ED leaderboard comparison; relevant to the broad “TFM data cleaning” claim. |
| Robust cellwise estimation | Identify or downweight individual cells under statistical contamination models | DDC, CellMCD, CellGMM, robust cellwise regression | Directly relevant to cell localization, but data types, assumptions, outputs, and benchmarks may differ. |

Do not combine Cell-ED AP/precision-recall with row-level AUROC, F1, power, or false-positive rate into a single SOTA ranking.

## CRIC-ED protocol versus evaluated alternatives

The current protocol describes unsupervised Cell-ED: score each eligible cell by predicting its target column from other columns, with the target excluded from its features. It uses five outer row folds; for the purification arm, nested first-pass scores are computed only within each outer training pool, and the top-scored context rows for each target column are removed. The main contrast is TabPFN-2 purification versus removing the same number of rows uniformly at random. Context preparation and extra compute are reported. A secondary raw leave-entity-out analysis is limited to tables with declared observed keys.

The study pins TabPFN-2 under its approved protocol for reproducibility. That is not a claim that v2 is the newest or strongest available backbone as of 2026; 2026 anomaly/context papers evaluate newer PFN generations. Backbone freshness, license, and task-specific SOTA are separate questions and the frozen checkpoint must not be silently replaced.

This makes the central novelty candidate **more specific than “influence trimming”**. The implemented operation is a one-step suspicion-ranked removal of whole context rows, target by target; it is not a gradient-based influence estimator, iterative trimming, guaranteed protection of rare-valid cells, or conformal calibration. Current paper text appropriately avoids those claims.

| Comparison layer | What the current setup can test | What it cannot establish by itself |
|---|---|---|
| TabPFN-2 purification vs matched-count random removal | Whether this fixed ranking rule differs from random context reduction on each named table under the declared protocol. | That it beats all context selectors, is universally robust, or improves unseen tables. |
| TabPFN-2 raw vs LightGBM raw | Difference between these pinned implementations under the shared scoring/data protocol. | A pure causal “foundation weights” effect: feature handling differs, and LightGBM is task-trained. |
| Purified vs raw context | Effect of the declared removal operation relative to retaining all sampled rows. | That every removed row was erroneous, or that the intervention isolates every causal mechanism. |
| Clean-context oracle | Diagnostic gap to a context whose values are replaced with clean values. | A deployable baseline, guaranteed upper bound, or fair SOTA competitor. |
| Row-fold vs observed-key leave-entity-out raw scoring | Sensitivity to this alternate boundary on three keyed tables. | Fully inductive generalization or true entity independence when keys are noisy/ambiguous. |
| Frequency, robust numeric, Isolation Forest, DDCcore | Whether simple marginal, numerical, or conditional baselines explain observed behavior. | State-of-the-art ranking against reference DDC, CDC, uTabPFN, ZeroED, ForestED, or RFOD. |
| Auto-Test and LUCARIO | Whether learned semantic-domain constraints or inferred pattern/range rules can detect the same benchmark errors. | Neither is currently an executed baseline in this protocol; no ranking is available. |

## Recommended comparison plan before making a novelty or SOTA claim

1. **Complete the closest-method audit from primary sources.** Reconcile the full LUCARIO methods and benchmark protocol against Uni-Detect, Auto-Test, ZeroED, ForestED, Raha, and CDC; inspect the complete evaluation sections for RFOD and the MICCAI cross-modal paper; record data, row/cell labels, scoring unit, split, metric, thresholding, and whether code/weights are available. Verify Cell-TRICC's count-data scope, the current ForestED record/version, and final CellMCD bibliographic details before manuscript submission.
2. **Add a same-task Cell-ED table.** Prioritize Auto-Test and LUCARIO as direct unsupervised comparators, and Raha if its supervision budget can be made comparable, where fixed data and metric permit; compare only on a shared eligible-cell universe and same dirty/clean labels, with identical row caps, folds, seeds, coverage handling, and metric. Include reference DDC or label it as unavailable; do not label DDCcore as DDC. Assess whether CDC, uTabPFN, ZeroED, or ForestED can actually be run under the $0/local authorization and their data/model terms.
3. **Keep row-anomaly work as contextual related work.** RFOD, TACTIC, ZEN/FOCUS and MICCAI cross-modal screening help delimit method novelty, but do not import their reported row-level metrics into the Cell-ED leaderboard.
4. **Inspect implementation and licenses separately from paper claims.** Pin code commits, checkpoints and weights; record unavailable or incompatible baselines rather than silently replacing them with proxies.
5. **Wait for complete artifact-backed evaluation.** Verify every frozen run, split, source/checkpoint hash, unsupported-cell denominator and failure mask; compare full-grid analyses and an independent analysis replay before adding any performance conclusion. Synthetic fixtures remain software validation only.
6. **Bound the novelty sentence to the evidence.** If results support it, describe the contribution as an empirical study of a fixed, nested, target-specific context-row purification rule for Cell-ED, evaluated against matched random removal on named benchmarks. Avoid “first,” “SOTA,” “influence-aware,” rare-valid protection, or general guarantees unless separate evidence directly establishes them.

### Update after targeted recency check (2026-10-31)

A focused search for 2026 cell-level detectors identified **LUCARIO**, published in *Information and Software Technology*, volume 192 (April 2026). It was absent from the first draft of this comparison and should be treated as a high-priority direct comparator. The source's abstract reports an average F1 of 0.54 on six open datasets and superiority over its tested unsupervised baselines; this is a paper-reported result on a different evaluation, not a result comparable to CRIC-ED's AP without an aligned experiment. The expanded search also surfaced **Auto-Test** (SIGMOD 2025), a direct unsupervised error detector based on semantic-domain constraints, **MaTElDa** (EDBT 2025), a semi-supervised multi-table cell detector, and **Uni-Detect** (SIGMOD 2019), an automated corpus-based detector. They are close in task but differ in supervision, corpus requirements, and evaluation. **Cell-TRICC** (online April 2026) confirms that cellwise trimming is itself established, although its count-matrix co-clustering algorithm is distinct from CRIC-ED's context-row selection.

The search also identified **Test-Time Compute for Tabular Foundation Models** (October 2026), which studies context retrieval and construction for modern TFMs. This is adjacent evidence that context selection is active and task-dependent, but it does not evaluate cell-error detection and is not a direct performance baseline.

Search also returned **Trail, “Revisiting weakly supervised tabular anomaly detection from a cell-level perspective,”** with a publisher date of November 2026. Because that date is after this document's review cutoff (2026-10-31), it is **not counted as published/current work as of this review date**. Recheck it in a later literature update; do not backdate it into the current closest-work evidence. [Publisher record](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0893608026006635).
