Context Puriﬁcation for Cell-Level Error
Detection with Tabular Foundation Models
Ngo Dang Trong Hieu[0009−0007−1773−3680]
Ton Duc Thang University
ngodangtronghieu.dl2021@gmail.com
Abstract. Conditional prediction can score individual cells of a dirty ta-
ble, but the reference examples used by a tabular foundation model can
themselves be corrupted. We evaluate a ﬁxed puriﬁcation rule that re-
moves context rows with the largest nested out-of-fold target-cell scores,
with all ﬁrst-pass work isolated from the outer query fold. A paired
random-removal control holds base contexts and removal counts ﬁxed,
and nested preparation costs are reported explicitly. The study covers
four capped benchmark tables, real and two injected-error conditions,
ﬁve seeds, and both TabPFN-2 and LightGBM. On real errors, puriﬁca-
tion was worse than matched random removal on three tables and better
on one (TabPFN AP diﬀerence −0.161 on hospital, −0.049 on ﬂights,
+0.026 on beers and −0.030 on rayyan), and no detector, including the
simple baselines, was best on every table. These are per-table ﬁndings
with conditional row-bootstrap uncertainty, not a population-wide ver-
dict. We also report support, failures, rare-valid alarms, boundary sensi-
tivity and compute on the complete eligible-cell universe. The results ar-
gue for testing context selection against matched random removal rather
than assuming that a cleaner-looking context helps.
Keywords: Cell error detection · Tabular foundation models · Con-
text puriﬁcation · Contaminated context.

Introduction
Data errors can aﬀect only a few attributes of a record. A suspicious product
measurement does not imply that its name and producer are wrong, and a rare
bibliographic entry need not contain an error. This makes cell localization a dif-
ferent objective from ranking entire rows as anomalies. Robust cellwise methods
already exploit relationships among variables [16]; a foundation model does not
make that problem new. The question addressed here is whether the context used
by a conditional predictor can be improved without access to clean examples or
error labels.
A pretrained tabular model oﬀers a practical route to conditional scoring.
Treat each column in turn as a prediction target, use the remaining columns
as features, and score the observed target value under the resulting prediction.
TabPFN supports classiﬁcation and regression through in-context prediction [5].

In a dirty table, however, the reference examples are themselves imperfect. Re-
peated corrupt values may become predictable, while infrequent correct values
can receive large suspicion scores. Removing the highest-scoring context cells’
rows could therefore either improve the reference distribution or erase legitimate
variation. Neither outcome follows from the model’s supervised prediction capa-
bility.
We study this intervention under a controlled cell-detection protocol. For
each target column and outer query fold, a nested ﬁrst pass scores only the
outer training pool. The puriﬁcation arm removes context rows with the largest
target-cell scores. A random arm removes exactly the same number of rows from
the same base context. Both retain the same queries and evaluation denominator,
which separates targeted selection from simply reducing context size; the extra
ﬁrst-pass computation is reported separately.
The empirical scope is deliberately speciﬁc: four capped benchmark tables,
their real errors, two controlled corruption conditions and ﬁve seeds. Real-error
puriﬁcation is primary; injections and an observed-key leave-entity-out analysis
are probes. Eﬀects are reported per source table; repeated conditions and seeds
are not treated as additional independent datasets.
The study contributes an outer-fold-isolated implementation of a simple con-
text intervention, a matched-count and explicitly costed comparison against ran-
dom removal, and an artifact-backed assessment spanning accuracy, rare-valid
alarms, support, failures and boundary sensitivity. It does not introduce a new
pretrained model, an inﬂuence estimator, or a conformal guarantee. The breadth
of any empirical conclusion is limited to the measured implementations and
named tables.

Related Work and Scope
Cellwise detection and imputation. DDC uses robust relationships between nu-
merical variables to predict cells and assess their residuals [16]. Its published
algorithm includes robust standardization, bivariate relations, prediction and
deshrinkage. Our numerical DDCcore baseline is a simpler comparator and can-
not establish a ranking against reference DDC. CellGMM addresses numerical
populations with a mixture model and a detection–imputation step [17]; discard-
ing an entire record can lose useful information from its other cells. Our removal
operates on whole context rows for one target at a time and oﬀers no correspond-
ing mixture-estimation or cell-repair guarantee. Cellwise trimming has also been
built into model-based co-clustering [4], so trimming as such is not new here.
Conformal Data Cleaning (CDC) combines column prediction with calibrated
prediction sets or intervals [7]. Its evaluated deployment scenario trains on clean
data and subsequently encounters corrupted inputs. Our deployment arms re-
ceive one dirty table and no clean calibration split. The LightGBM conditional
comparator is inspired by column prediction but does not reproduce CDC, and
our scores have no conformal coverage interpretation.

Foundation models and contaminated contexts. TabPFN provides pretrained
conditional prediction for small tabular problems [5]. We use pinned second-
generation weights through an explicit adapter, rather than whichever model
a current package would select by default. Unsupervised extensions of TabPFN
also use conditional prediction; we do not claim this reduction as novel or call our
raw arm a reproduction of the extensions. TACTIC explicitly studies contami-
nated contexts for row anomaly detection [11]. Its anomaly-oriented pretraining
and row-level evaluation address a related robustness problem. Consequently,
context contamination itself is established prior work; our intervention concerns
cell-level rankings under ﬁxed pretrained weights and nested selection, not a
row-AUROC comparison.
Data quality and context selection for TFMs. QualiTab evaluates imperfect
data and contrasts perfect-data, perfect-context and no-intervention settings for
TabPFN embeddings [14]. Its clean-context comparison is close to the motivation
of our oracle diagnostic; the beneﬁt of cleaner contexts is therefore not a new
hypothesis introduced here. Model-aware cleaning learns reinforcement-learning
policies that sequence cleaning operators for TFM-oriented data preparation [2].
We instead evaluate one ﬁxed removal rule as a cell-ranking intervention without
learning a cleaning policy. LUCoS selects representative contexts using unsuper-
vised PFN embeddings, then obtains labels for supervised prediction [6]. Our
contexts contain observed target values from the dirty table, with no annota-
tion oracle for deployment. A study of test-time compute for TFMs reports that
attention-guided row retrieval helps on some large tables but can fail badly, for
example by distorting class composition, and that context expansion gives no
consistent gain [13]. It concerns supervised prediction from labelled contexts, not
detecting errors in a dirty context, so it is related evidence rather than a direct
comparator. Together these works rule out broad novelty claims about TFM
data cleaning, label-free context selection or context selection in general.
Systems for heterogeneous error detection. Raha is a conﬁguration-free error-
detection system and supplies the benchmark repository used here [10]. Auto-
Test learns semantic-domain constraints from a large table corpus oﬄine and
applies them to detect erroneous column values without per-table supervision [3].
MaTElDa is semi-supervised: it groups cells of several tables by domain and
quality similarity so that a small labelling budget can be shared across tables [1].
LUCARIO targets unsupervised, lightweight detection of pattern anomalies and
outliers [15]. These are direct cell-level competitors with diﬀerent knowledge
sources (a table corpus, user labels, inferred constraints); none was run under
our protocol. We use its paired dirty/clean tables, not a reimplementation of
its complete detection workﬂow. ZeroED combines LLM-derived representations
and labels with detector training [12]. That information and compute regime
diﬀers from our label-free local deployment arms. Neither system’s published
headline score is an interchangeable baseline result for our capped-table protocol.

They test whether marginal rarity, simple numerical deviation or the choice of

Conditional Scoring and Context Puriﬁcation
3.1
Task and information boundary
Let e
X be an observed table with n rows and d eligible columns. The detector
outputs S ∈Rn×d, with larger values indicating greater suspicion. Evaluation
compares observed strings with aligned clean strings; this deﬁnes benchmark
disagreement, rather than a universal semantic deﬁnition of an error. Clean val-
ues and the error matrix are unavailable to deployment arms. Only a separately
labelled oracle receives clean contexts.
For seed s, partition rows into K = 5 outer folds Qf. Sample a sorted base
context Bf of at most C = 512 rows from {1, . . . , n} \ Qf. Share these bases
across target columns, removal arms and models. To score column j, drop j from
the features and predict its observed values from the remaining eligible columns.
Excluded identiﬁer columns are not conditioning features. The query fold never
enters a ﬁtted deployment context. Other rows of the same entity may be used
in the primary row-level analysis.
Typing and feature encoding are ﬁtted on the complete observed table for
the ﬁnal pass. Numerical features use parsed numbers with unparseable entries
as missing; other features use observed frequency-rank codes and an unknown
code. This is label-free transductive preprocessing: outer queries can aﬀect en-
coding, although they do not enter the model’s ﬁtted context. We distinguish
this assumption from a fully inductive ﬁt on context rows only.
3.2
Mixed-type suspicion scores

as ﬁnite numbers. Otherwise it is categorical, unless its observed unique-value
ratio exceeds 0.5, in which case the target is unsupported. Unsupported cells
receive score zero and remain in every whole-universe evaluation denominator.
They may still supply encoded conditioning features.

Relation to residual and conformal scores. DDC standardizes residuals from

deployment arms have no clean calibration split and the context may contain
errors, so S is used only as an uncalibrated ranking evaluated by precision–recall
metrics. Removal is based on ﬁrst-pass suspicion scores, not on a gradient-based
inﬂuence estimate.

matched-compute random cost is ordinary random time plus the same nested
preparation time, using unchanged random scores; it is not a separately timed
arm. No task-speciﬁc TabPFN weight update is performed; its context ﬁtting and
preprocessing remain chargeable work. LightGBM, in contrast, ﬁts task-speciﬁc
trees for each context.

Experimental Protocol
4.1
Data, roles and corruption conditions
We use a pinned snapshot of the Raha benchmark repository, upstream commit
7be1334b8c7bbdac3f47ef514fb3e1e8c5fc181c, with per-ﬁle hashes. All CSV
values are read as strings, retaining empty strings. An eligible cell is an entry
outside a uniquely valued column named id, index or tuple_id, with eligibil-
ity established on the source observed table. The error label is an exact string
disagreement with the aligned reference. Unsupported targets remain eligible;
successful-output-only evaluation would otherwise reward methods for avoiding
diﬃcult columns.

movies_1 is development data and does not enter conﬁrmatory summaries.
The conﬁrmatory collection comprises hospital, flights, beers and rayyan.
Each table is capped at 1,000 rows before injection: tables below the cap retain
every row; larger tables use a single seed-0 sample without replacement, sorted
in original row order. Identical source rows and column order are used across
conditions, models and seeds. The study targets these capped tables, not their
uncapped distributions.
Each table has three conditions. Real retains the observed values. Random
injection starts from the clean reference and attempts corruption at individually
sampled eligible positions. Systematic injection also starts from the reference but
repeats a shared wrong value across sampled occurrences of the same column
value. Both target a 10% corruption rate. Attempted error types are sampled uni-
formly from character edits, replacement by another column value, empty-string
replacement, format changes and numeric rescaling. Rejected or unchanged pro-
posals imply that realized type proportions are not uniform. A ﬁxed attempt
guard could produce a shortfall, but all 40 injected units reached exactly the
10% target. These are string-level mechanism probes, not claims to reproduce
every natural error process.
Run seeds are 0, 1, 2, 3, 4. The injection and context algorithms both use the
run seed; their randomness is coupled rather than independently crossed. The
primary contrast averages the ﬁve paired AP diﬀerences separately for each real
table. Corruption conditions remain separate repeated measurements within a
source table.
4.2
Models and comparators
TabPFN uses package version 9.2.0 and explicit second-generation classiﬁer and
regressor checkpoint paths. The pinned upstream revisions are recorded with
checkpoint and license hashes in the reproducibility manifest. We use one es-
timator, CPU inference and the run seed for model randomness. Package and
weight licenses are evaluated separately; later package defaults or checkpoint
generations are not substituted.
The conditional LightGBM model uses 100 trees, learning rate 0.1, 15 leaves,
minimum child size ﬁve and one training thread. Classiﬁcation predicts the con-
text target classes. Regression uses a Gaussian tail about its predicted mean,
with scale estimated from within-context cross-validated residuals. Thus the
conditional-model comparison includes diﬀerent distributional approximations,
not just a backbone replacement with equivalent calibration.
Value frequency (VF) scores smoothed leave-one-out token frequency in each
column. Pattern frequency (PF) applies this rule to character-class signatures.
RobustZ scores numerical deviations using median and MAD with the declared
fallback; nonnumerical columns receive zero. ColumnIF ﬁts 100 Isolation Forest
trees separately to each column, using numbers or frequency encodings. DDCcore
combines robust numerical standardization, selected pairwise relationships and
residual scores. It omits parts of reference DDC and is reported under its own

For each paired contrast, draw 1,000 row-bootstrap samples with seed 2027.
Use the same resampled row IDs for both arms and every run seed, retaining all
cells in each selected row. Recalculate the ﬁve paired AP diﬀerences and their
mean. Discard the whole bootstrap draw if any constituent seed has no positive
errors, and report the valid-draw count. Percentiles give the descriptive 95%
interval. These intervals condition on ﬁtted predictions and declared seeds. They
exclude retraining and new-table uncertainty, and can understate uncertainty
when rows share entities. Cross-ﬁtting and the LEO diagnostic do not make row
resampling independent.
Secondary contrasts compare raw TabPFN with raw LightGBM, oracle with
raw, puriﬁcation with raw, raw-LEO with row-level raw, and LightGBM puriﬁ-
cation with its random control. Equal-weight macro means summarize named
source tables separately for each condition. There is no pooled-cell cross-table
endpoint, Wilcoxon/Holm decision or binary superiority verdict. Four sources
do not become twelve independent datasets by repeating three conditions.

Empirical Results
5.1
Completeness, support and primary comparison
All 60 declared table–condition–seed units completed with both models, and the
saved artifacts passed input, prediction and context-boundary checks. Prediction
failures occurred in one unit only, hospital/random injection/seed 4. There,
TabPFN raised 435 caught input-validation errors (non-ﬁnite encoded values)
in the ﬁnal pass of every TabPFN arm and in the nested ﬁrst pass; LightGBM,
the other 59 units and all real-error units had none. The failures cover 54% of
that unit’s cells for raw, 53% for puriﬁcation, 51% for random and 15% for the
oracle. Following the protocol, these cells keep their place in the denominator
with a tied maximal score; the raw, random and puriﬁcation APs of that unit
fall to about 0.11, close to its 10% error rate. Pass-1 failures receive the same
penalty, so 76% of the rows removed in that unit’s successful puriﬁcation ﬁts
were selected by the penalty rather than by suspicion scores. Section 5.4 reports
a post hoc check. A separate ﬂights/real/seed-0 rerun reproduced every saved
array and context record exactly; it is excluded from performance summaries.
This is a same-implementation rerun, not an independent replication. Table 1
shows the real-data universe and conditional support.

Limitations and Reproducibility
The collection contains four capped source tables, so per-table comparisons can
expose heterogeneous eﬀects but not population-wide superiority. Descriptive
data access preceded the amended speciﬁcation, which limits claims of an un-
touched benchmark. Exact reference disagreement includes formatting changes
and possible ambiguity in benchmark corrections. Our corruptions start from
the clean reference, may be easier than natural errors and share the seed with
context sampling; they are mechanism probes, not substitutes for the real-error
experiment. The oracle changes all context features, so its gap does not isolate
target-cell contamination.
The ﬁnal encoder observes all dirty rows, and the primary analysis allows
other records of the same entity in the context. Observed-key LEO cannot repair
key errors or stop the encoder from seeing held-out rows. Row-bootstrap intervals
may be too narrow under dependence, omit retraining and new-table variation,
and are not multiplicity-controlled.
Numerical tail scores and categorical probabilities are not proven equally cali-
brated. LightGBM treats frequency-rank codes as numerical covariates, whereas
TabPFN receives categorical indices, so the model contrast also reﬂects feature
handling. Marginal baselines use whole observed columns, while conditional mod-
els exclude query folds. Whole-universe penalties keep unsupported and failed
cells in the denominator, but do not make support equivalent; in one injected
unit the failure penalty covered about half of all TabPFN cells and reversed one
secondary contrast. Rare-valid alarm rates cover a reference-deﬁned subset and
do not establish protection of legitimate minority values.
Puriﬁcation is one suspicion-based rule at one removal fraction and context
cap; it is neither an inﬂuence function nor a robustness theorem. The accounted
matched-compute control does not test whether another use of the same compute,
such as context ensembling, would help. Other selection strategies, context-size
sweeps and clean-calibration regimes are outside this study. DDCcore coincided
with RobustZ on every real-error unit and adds no evidence about relational

Conclusion
We evaluated a simple context-puriﬁcation intervention for conditional cell-error
scoring under an explicitly transductive row-level protocol. On the four named
real-error tables, the paired AP diﬀerence to random removal was −0.161 on
hospital, −0.049 on ﬂights, +0.026 on beers and −0.030 on rayyan, although
a clean-context oracle showed room for improvement on every table. The de-
clared rule therefore did not exploit that headroom in general. Nested isolation,
complete denominators, failure masks and explicit preparation cost were needed
to interpret these results; they do not provide a robustness theorem or a cross-
dataset superiority claim. Broader conclusions require more datasets, other con-
text strategies, direct cell-level competitors and dependence-aware validation.
