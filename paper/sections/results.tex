\section{Empirical Results}
\subsection{Completeness, support and primary comparison}
All 60 declared table--condition--seed units completed with both models, and the
saved artifacts passed input, prediction and context-boundary checks. Prediction
failures occurred in one unit only, \texttt{hospital}/random injection/seed~4.
There, TabPFN raised 435 caught input-validation errors (non-finite encoded values)
in the final pass of every TabPFN arm and in the nested first pass; LightGBM, the
other 59 units and all real-error units had none. The failures cover 54\% of that
unit's cells for raw, 53\% for purification, 51\% for random and 15\% for the oracle.
Following the protocol, these cells keep their place in the denominator with a tied
maximal score; the raw, random and purification APs of that unit fall to about 0.11,
close to its 10\% error rate.
Section~\ref{sec:sens} reports a post hoc sensitivity check.
A separate flights/real/seed-0 rerun reproduced every saved array and context record
exactly; it is excluded from performance summaries. This is a same-implementation
rerun, not an independent replication.
Table~\ref{tab:coverage} shows the real-data universe and conditional support.

The primary contrast compares purification with random removal on each real table,
averaging paired AP differences over all five seeds. Purification was worse than
random removal on three tables (hospital $-0.1611$, flights $-0.0493$, rayyan $-0.0300$)
and better on beers ($+0.0257$); all four descriptive intervals exclude zero.
Table~\ref{tab:primary} gives the APs. The unweighted four-table mean is $-0.0537$,
reported without a test or interval. The intervals condition on saved predictions;
they do not resolve dependence among related rows or uncertainty across new tables.
On real errors, therefore, the declared rule did not generally improve on removing
the same number of randomly chosen rows.

\subsection{Comparators and information boundary}
Table~\ref{tab:baselines} reports baseline APs on the same complete eligible universe.
No method was best on every table. Raw TabPFN had the highest real-error AP on hospital
(0.671, closely followed by column-wise Isolation Forest at 0.663), whereas pattern
frequency was highest on flights and beers and conditional LightGBM on rayyan.
Frequency-based methods can score targets that the conditional pipeline declares
unsupported, while RobustZ and DDCcore have numerical support only. On all 20
real-error units, DDCcore returned scores identical to RobustZ, because no numerical
column pair passed its correlation threshold (flights has no numerical column);
it differed in only 10 of the 40 injected units. DDCcore and conditional LightGBM are
separate implementations, not reproduced DDC or CDC systems.
\input{generated/baselines}

Secondary differences in Table~\ref{tab:secondary} distinguish conditional-model,
oracle-context and observed-key boundary effects. The oracle changes context targets
and conditioning features together and retains the dirty-fitted encoder, so its gap
need not be positive and is not a theoretical upper bound. It was positive on every
real table, indicating headroom from cleaner contexts that the declared rule did not
recover. LEO uses different bases, excludes observed keys only, and keeps transductive
final encoding; its large drop on hospital suggests that the row-level primary analysis
benefits from same-entity rows in the context. These contrasts diagnose sensitivity
rather than isolate a single causal explanation.

\subsection{Injection probes and rare-valid alarms}
Table~\ref{tab:probes} shows the two injection conditions.
They remain separate from the real-error estimand: repeated wrong-value templates
and independently selected string corruptions need not resemble natural error
processes, and their seeds are coupled to context randomness. Under systematic
injection, purification was slightly better than random removal on all four tables
(at most $+0.025$); under random injection the effect ranged from $-0.137$ (flights)
to $+0.131$ (hospital). The hospital random-injection value contains the failed unit.
\input{generated/probes}

Table~\ref{tab:rare} reports rare-valid alarm fractions at the reference-error-count
budget, with the valid-cell denominator. This is an evaluation-only operating point.
Purification lowered the alarm fraction on hospital, beers and rayyan and raised it
on flights; on hospital, the reduction coincided with a large AP loss, so fewer
rare-valid alarms do not by themselves indicate a better detector. No rare-valid
mask was provided to purification. The seed-level export retains R-precision,
precision at 1\% and 5\%, type-specific AP, support and undefined-endpoint counts.

\subsection{Post hoc sensitivity to the failed unit}\label{sec:sens}
After inspecting the results, we recomputed the affected random-injection contrasts
from the saved scores in two ways that were not preregistered: excluding seed~4, and
evaluating seed~4 only on cells where neither compared arm failed (8,800 of 19,000
cells, error rate 9.9\% versus 10.1\% among failed cells). The protocol values remain
the reported results. Purification minus random was $+0.131$ under the protocol,
$+0.163$ over seeds~0--3 and $+0.138$ with failed cells dropped; the oracle gap was
$+0.220$, $+0.242$ and $+0.229$. The sign of raw TabPFN minus raw LightGBM changed:
$-0.094$ under the protocol, $-0.0002$ over seeds~0--3 and $+0.016$ with failed cells
dropped. The failures thus do not create the hospital purification gain under random
injection, but the protocol value of the model contrast in that condition reflects
the failures rather than model ranking. Real-error results are unaffected.

\subsection{Compute and scope of interpretation}
Table~\ref{tab:cost} separates nested preparation from final purification work.
The nested component is included in the inclusive purification time, and adding it
to ordinary random time produces the declared accounted matched-compute control
with unchanged random scores. It is not a separately timed accuracy arm. Wall times
come from concurrent local CPU execution. The grid was interrupted twice and resumed
without changing code, inputs or threads, and some units ran while the operating
system throttled background processes. Timings therefore span three execution
periods: the TabPFN raw time on hospital/real, for example, ranged from 6.8 to 46.2
minutes across seeds. The table supports order-of-magnitude statements only;
within a unit, nested preparation was the dominant cost of purification.
Peak memory and actual neural forward-call counts were not measured.
\input{generated/cost}

These conclusions concern this fixed removal rule, the pinned weights, the
support policy and the capped collection. The real-error contrasts and injection probes
must be read together with baseline performance and rare-valid alarms. They do not
establish that context purification generally succeeds or fails, that clean contexts
always help, or that an equal budget could not be better spent on another strategy.
No outcome was used to revise the frozen design or select a subset of seeds.
