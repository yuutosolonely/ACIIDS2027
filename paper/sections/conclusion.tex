\section{Conclusion}
We evaluated a simple context-purification intervention for conditional cell-error
scoring under an explicitly transductive row-level protocol. On the four named
real-error tables, the paired AP difference to random removal was
$-0.161$ on hospital, $-0.049$ on flights, $+0.026$ on beers and $-0.030$ on rayyan, although a
clean-context oracle showed room for improvement on every table. The declared rule
therefore did not exploit that headroom in general. Nested isolation, complete
denominators, failure masks and explicit preparation cost were needed to interpret
these results; they do not provide a robustness theorem or a cross-dataset
superiority claim. Broader conclusions require more datasets, other context
strategies, direct cell-level competitors and dependence-aware validation.
