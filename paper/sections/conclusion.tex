\section{Conclusion}
We evaluated a simple context-purification intervention for conditional cell-error
scoring under an explicitly transductive row-level protocol. On real errors, removing
the most suspicious context rows was worse than removing the same number of random
rows on three of the four tables, although a clean-context oracle showed room for
improvement on every table; the declared rule did not exploit that headroom in general.
Nested isolation, complete denominators, failure masks and explicit preparation cost
were needed to interpret these results, which provide neither a robustness theorem nor
a cross-dataset superiority claim. Broader conclusions require more datasets, other
context strategies, direct cell-level competitors and dependence-aware validation.
